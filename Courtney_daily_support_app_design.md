# Understory — Daily Support App Design Ledger

**Working title:** Understory

**Status:** Research and co-design. No implementation authorised yet.

## Purpose

Create a private, low-friction daily support app for emotional regulation, reality-checking, identity continuity, and manageable goal support. It must not feel like a chore or pretend to diagnose, treat, or replace professional care.

## Confirmed priorities

### Primary difficult-day job

The first thing the app should help Courtney do is:

> **Calm her body and stop an emotional spiral.**

Goal planning, journalling, analysis, and identity work should not obstruct access to immediate regulation.

### Spiral presentation

Courtney's physical response changes and may combine:

- Panic, racing heart, tightness, and urgency.
- Anger, heat, agitation, and restlessness.
- Numbness, unreality, floating, or disconnection.

The app must not assume every spiral is the same type of anxiety.

## Proposed front door

The home screen should offer one obvious action:

> **I'm spiralling**

This should start a short, mostly tap-based flow:

1. Ask what is loudest now: panic, agitation, disconnection, mixed, or unknown.
2. Offer one 30–90 second body-based regulation action matched to that state.
3. Check whether intensity changed at all, without demanding complete calm.
4. Offer a different intervention if needed.
5. Only after some regulation, offer reality-checking, an unsent-message hold, a clear request for human support, one manageable next action, or permission to rest.

During severe activation, minimise reading, typing, choices, analysis, and clinical language.

## Intended broader jobs

1. **Bring me back:** grounding and body regulation.
2. **Help me reality-check:** distinguish fact, interpretation, want, fear, and reasonable request.
3. **Help me remember myself:** surface values, preferences, boundaries, identity anchors, and notes from calmer states.
4. **Help me take one step:** convert vague goals into one manageable action based on available capacity.

## Early interaction principles

- Large, clear controls during activation.
- Calm visuals and minimal text.
- Optional audio or haptic pacing where supported.
- An explicit “I don't know” path.
- Learn which tools Courtney reports as useful; do not repeatedly force techniques that make things worse.
- Support variable capacity: barely functioning, ordinary, or higher-capacity days.
- Do not require daily use.

## Safety boundaries

The app must not become:

- A diagnostic tool.
- A replacement for therapy, assessment, emergency care, or trusted people.
- A reassurance vending machine.
- A relationship-surveillance or partner-monitoring tool.
- An engine for repeated rumination or compulsive interpretation.
- A punitive streak system.
- A guilt-producing mandatory check-in.
- An endless symptom journal.

If Courtney reports genuine difficulty knowing what is real, the app should use simple orientation prompts and encourage contact with a trusted person or urgent support when appropriate. It must not present itself as the sole authority on reality.

## Evidence review

### Clinical framing

- The app must not diagnose CPTSD, BPD, dissociation, psychosis, relapse, or relationship danger.
- CPTSD is a distinct ICD-11 diagnosis involving PTSD symptoms plus problems with emotional regulation, negative self-concept, and relationships. DSM-5-TR does not list it separately.
- BPD can involve emotion regulation, identity, relationship, abandonment, impulsivity, and transient dissociation or suspiciousness, but these features overlap with many other conditions and require professional assessment.
- High emotional load can temporarily reduce access to attention, working memory, planning, and interpersonal skills. The app should therefore become simpler as distress increases.
- Structured psychotherapy is the main evidence-based treatment. An app may reinforce skills and daily functioning but must not claim to provide comprehensive DBT, MBT, or trauma-focused therapy.
- Because Courtney has described occasionally struggling to determine what feels real, the app must distinguish ordinary interpretation-checking from severe confusion, hallucination-like experiences, major time loss, mania, intoxication, neurological symptoms, or inability to remain safe. Those require human clinical or urgent support rather than automated analysis.

### Confirmed product structure from research

Use three capacity layers:

1. **Now:** 30 seconds to three minutes for orienting, grounding, delaying impulsive action, or reaching human help.
2. **Today:** one stabilising, self-care, household, connection, or goal-resumption action.
3. **Explore:** identity, values, patterns, relationships, and longer-term goals when Courtney has capacity.

Lead with lived problems rather than therapy terminology:

- I'm spiralling.
- I feel angry and may act.
- I feel unreal or far away.
- I'm about to send something I may regret.
- I can't tell what I need.
- I can't start.
- Help me return to my day.

### Courtney's current regulation experience

- Courtney does not currently identify a body-based technique that reliably helps.
- Her usual way of getting through intense states is to remain distracted.
- Treat distraction as potentially valid distress tolerance rather than a failed attempt to become calm.
- The app should distinguish **turn the volume down** from **carry me through the peak**.
- Success may mean delaying an impulsive action, staying safe, or returning to ordinary life even when the feeling has not disappeared.
- Intentional distraction should have a gentle return path so it does not become indefinite avoidance.
- Do not recommend compulsive relationship checking, rereading messages, surveillance, substance use, reckless behaviour, or other distractions likely to increase distress or harm.

Courtney currently uses:

- **Gaming:** high absorption and interruption of repetitive thought.
- **Watching something:** low-effort containment.
- **Crochet:** hand activity, repetition, attention, and sensory contact.
- **Cleaning:** movement, visible progress, and an outlet for agitation.
- **Sleep:** recovery when depleted, while recognising it can sometimes function as escape.
- **Scrolling:** immediate and accessible, but at higher risk of extending avoidance or exposing her to destabilising content.

Present these by available capacity rather than by clinical category:

- Hands busy.
- Body moving.
- Mind absorbed.
- Very low energy.
- Sleep.

Show only two or three relevant options at once. Do not moralise about scrolling or sleep. Where technically possible and explicitly configured, the app may deep-link to selected games, media, playlists, or timers. After distraction, offer a neutral return choice: **keep resting, check what I need, or return to one small thing.**

### State-specific regulation direction

The first prompt remains **What is loudest right now?** with:

- Panic / too much energy.
- Anger / urge to act.
- Frozen / shut down.
- Disconnected / unreal.
- Spiralling thoughts.
- Mixed.
- I don't know.

Requirements:

- One tap to begin.
- No mandatory typing, account, internet, countdown, distress rating, or completion ritual.
- Large targets, literal language, low visual density, and visible skip/exit controls.
- Text, visual, and optional audio alternatives.
- Ask consent before breath, body-focus, eyes-closed, or sensory exercises; these can worsen panic or dissociation for some people.
- Offer 10-second, two-minute, and more-support versions.
- Record **helped / neutral / made worse** only when Courtney chooses, and allow any exercise to be hidden permanently.

### Identity and goals

- Maintain a user-authored **Things that remain true about me** collection: values, preferences, roles, strengths, interests, boundaries, current priorities, and messages written during calmer states.
- Never infer a true personality, diagnosis, attachment style, motive, or identity from usage.
- Treat identity as flexible and discoverable rather than demanding one perfectly coherent self-description.
- Support goals through one visible next action, full/small/survival versions, neutral acknowledgement, and **resume** rather than restart.
- Useful prompts include: **What would make today 5% more inhabitable?** and **What is the smallest version that still respects your intention?**
- Incomplete or abandoned tasks must not be interpreted as failure, deterioration, or noncompliance.

### Courtney's identity-support needs

Courtney reports losing access to all of the following during difficult periods:

- Interests and preferences.
- Values and opinions.
- Goals and sense of future.
- A sense of self outside her relationship.

The app should not demand that she define a complete, permanent identity during onboarding. Build identity continuity gradually through optional, event-based captures:

- I liked this.
- I disliked this.
- This mattered to me.
- I chose this for myself.
- I handled this.
- I want to explore this.
- This is a boundary of mine.
- This is what I'm working toward.

The **Things that remain true about me** view may include Courtney's own words, interests, photos, music, creative projects, boundaries, past decisions, strengths, and messages written during calmer states. Keep the default retrieval view short enough to use while distressed.

The identity area should feel like a **private scrapbook**, using photos, colours, music, and personal notes rather than a clinical profile or plain task database. It may include:

- Visual memories, places, objects, aesthetics, and colours.
- Songs and playlists associated with different parts or periods of Courtney's life.
- Quotes and user-written sentences she wants returned to her.
- Interests she currently loves, previously loved, or wants to explore.
- Goals and unfinished projects without treating unfinished as dead.
- A dedicated **me outside my relationship** page.
- Optional dated layers so change does not overwrite or invalidate earlier versions.

Use two presentations of the same user-owned material:

- **Scrapbook:** rich, expressive, exploratory, and editable while calm.
- **Show me myself:** a minimal retrieval card containing a few preselected anchors during distress.

Calm Courtney can build the scrapbook; overwhelmed Courtney should never be asked to curate it.

Support uncertainty and change. **I'm exploring this** is a valid identity statement. The product must not demand internal consistency, freeze Courtney into a permanent profile, or treat changed preferences as instability or failure.

Include explicit space for identity outside the relationship without implying that attachment needs are shameful. Examples may include personally chosen interests, friendships, environments, skills, values, private goals, and ways she spends time alone.

### Courtney's goal-support needs

Courtney reports all four common goal-continuity failures:

- Forgetting that a goal exists.
- The goal becoming too large or complicated to begin.
- Mood changes making the goal stop feeling meaningful.
- A new interest or crisis replacing the goal.

Avoid a conventional overdue-task list. Represent each goal as a **thread** containing:

- **Why I chose this**, in Courtney's own calmer-state words.
- **Where I'm trying to go**, without requiring a complete project plan.
- **The next stitch:** one concrete, visible action.
- **Full, small, and survival-mode** versions.
- **Where I left off**, so resuming does not require reconstructing context.
- **Keep, change, pause, or release**, all treated as valid choices.
- A separate place to capture new interests without automatically replacing the current thread.

Nothing becomes overdue. A crisis can pause a thread without erasing it. A later return prompt may say: **You paused here. Do you want to continue, shrink it, change it, or leave it resting?**

Use optional, user-controlled reminders rather than nagging. Store the reason a goal mattered so a temporary mood is not its only judge, while still allowing Courtney to revise or release goals deliberately.

The home screen should **always show one gentle next step** from a chosen thread. It must include no due date, overdue state, streak consequence, or guilt language. Provide immediate controls such as:

- Start.
- Make smaller.
- Not today.

**Not today** should simply move the action aside without penalty. A survival-mode action may be as small as placing the needed materials within reach.

### Provisional home-screen hierarchy

1. **I'm spiralling:** immediate state-aware support.
2. **One gentle next step:** continuity with a chosen goal.
3. **Show me myself:** a condensed identity-scrapbook view for difficult moments.

Deeper reflection, browsing, editing, and planning should remain below these three anchors so the home screen does not become a dashboard cockpit.

### Reality-checking and relationships

When calm enough, separate:

1. Observable facts.
2. Emotions and body signals.
3. Interpretations.
4. Alternative possibilities.
5. The user's actual goal.
6. A proportionate next action.

- The app must repeatedly preserve uncertainty about other people's internal states.
- It must not declare that another person is abandoning, cheating, manipulating, abusing, or secretly thinking something.
- It may support concise **I feel / I need / I'm asking** messages, consent and timing checks, boundary planning, repair scripts, and delayed review of messages written while highly activated.
- It should encourage multiple sources of support rather than directing all distress toward one partner or toward the app.
- No message scraping, response-time scoring, covert location checking, or partner risk scores.

### First-version relationship and reality-check module

Include relationship and reality-check support in the initial version as **clarity and communication tools**, not automated adjudication.

#### I'm about to send something

- Compose or paste text locally.
- Offer **send now**, **hold for ten minutes**, or **come back when calmer**.
- Never send automatically.
- Never hard-block the user; the delay is supportive friction rather than parental control.
- Allow the user to delete the draft immediately and choose whether held drafts are retained.

#### What happened?

Use a short guided sequence:

1. What did I directly observe?
2. What meaning did my mind attach to it?
3. What am I feeling or noticing physically?
4. What do I need?
5. What am I actually trying to achieve?
6. What is one proportionate request or action?

Permit skipping any question and ending without saving.

#### Help me say it

Use editable, fixed, non-AI templates for:

- Asking for affection.
- Asking for clarity.
- Taking a pause with a specific return time.
- Setting a boundary.
- Repairing after an impulsive action.
- Raising a repeated pattern without absolute language.

#### Is this an old alarm, a current pattern, or both?

The app must not answer this question for Courtney. It may organise:

- Evidence suggesting an internal fear or prediction.
- Observable repeated behaviour.
- Previous explicit agreements and whether they occurred.
- What remains unknown.
- Whether the next need is regulation, a conversation, a boundary, or an outside human perspective.

Keep pattern capture brief and event-based. Do not create partner scores, danger scores, response-time analysis, or pseudo-clinical judgments. Do not dismiss legitimate external problems as symptoms or promote a frightened interpretation into established fact.

### Event-based useful memory

Default to event-based capture rather than daily compliance:

- Save this because it helped.
- Remember this trigger.
- This made things worse.
- This task was easier than expected.
- Show me what has helped in similar moments.

Summaries must be editable and cautious, for example: **Stepping outside and delaying difficult messages helped more often than breathing exercises.** Do not claim causation or treatment efficacy.

### Motivation

Allowed:

- Visible progress on user-chosen projects.
- Optional private collections of useful discoveries.
- Convenience or visual customisation unlocks.
- Welcoming return after absence.
- Neutral acknowledgement such as **Saved**, **Done**, or **That counted**.

Prohibited:

- Streak loss, leaderboards, social comparison, scarcity, countdowns, loot boxes, crisis points, XP for symptom disclosure, punitive avatars, sad virtual dependants, or guilt-based notifications.

### Privacy baseline

- Local-first storage where practical.
- No advertising SDKs, data sales, session replay, or third-party analytics by default.
- End-to-end encryption for any synchronised private content.
- Separate explicit consent for backups, research, sharing, or AI processing.
- In-app export, correction, and deletion.
- Per-entry controls for private, shareable, and excluded from AI.
- Generic notifications, optional neutral app name/icon, and biometric/app lock.
- A plain-language trust screen explaining what is stored, where, why, who can receive it, retention, and deletion.

### Safety limits

The app must not independently provide:

- Diagnosis or screening interpretation.
- Suicide or self-harm risk disposition.
- Medication decisions.
- Trauma processing, exposure, recovered-memory work, or detailed trauma prompts.
- Comprehensive DBT, MBT, or psychotherapy.
- Interpretation of dissociation versus psychosis, neurological illness, intoxication, mania, or other causes.
- Abuse or coercive-control adjudication.
- Automated outreach without explicit informed consent and a governed safety design.

It should contain a user-authored support plan, trusted contacts, professional-care details, and local urgent resources. Every acute screen needs an immediate exit.

### Product success measures

Do not optimise for daily active use, streaks, time in app, or number of disclosures. Prefer:

- Time from launch to a chosen regulation action.
- Whether acute sessions were completed or intentionally exited.
- Helped / neutral / made worse feedback.
- Ability to locate a preferred tool under high cognitive load.
- Returning to a chosen task or ordinary activity.
- Goal resumption after interruption.
- Trust, autonomy, burden, and unwanted notifications.
- Successful data export and deletion.
- Negative effects as well as benefits.

## Recommended delivery format

Start with a **phone-first Progressive Web App (PWA)** that can be installed to a home screen, used from a computer, and support the acute regulation flow offline.

Reasons:

- Fast phone access during activation.
- One cross-platform implementation.
- No app-store dependency for early testing.
- Lower-cost validation before committing to native development.
- Can later be wrapped or rebuilt as a native app if real use reveals a need.

Initial privacy architecture:

- Local-first and account-free.
- Core regulation tools available offline.
- No advertising, trackers, session replay, or cloud AI access to private entries.
- Manual encrypted backup/export because browser storage can be cleared or lost.
- Optional PIN/app locking where platform capabilities allow.
- No synchronisation in the initial version. Consider explicitly optional end-to-end encrypted sync only after the local product demonstrates value.

Initial notification policy:

- No notifications by default.
- No unsolicited emotional check-ins, inactivity prompts, engagement nudges, or inferred-risk notifications.
- Allow only reminders Courtney explicitly creates or enables.
- Keep lock-screen wording generic by default.
- Support one quiet goal prompt at a chosen time if explicitly enabled, without streaks, escalation, or repeated nagging.
- Every reminder must be individually snoozable, editable, and removable.

Trade-offs:

- PWA notification and biometric capabilities vary across devices.
- Local-only data requires clear backup and recovery design.
- This is acceptable because notifications are optional and should not be the product's engagement foundation.

### Help me understand what happened

Courtney reviewed the revised hosted flow and said it looked pretty good. Treat this as the **approved first version** of the understanding flow: suitable for integration and further functional development, while remaining open to later refinement through real use. The tested prototype is at `/root/.hermes/sketches/014-understand-what-happened-flow/index.html`, with documentation in the adjacent `README.md`. Verified behaviours include neutral text entry, forward/back navigation, Skip, I don’t know, multi-select internal states and needs, single-select next action, localStorage persistence, assembled summary, editing with answers preserved, restart, generated Binch context review, and copy-to-clipboard. Closing into the broader app and opening Telegram are visibly marked as not wired in this review prototype.

The interaction format is **one gentle question per screen**. Each step should use plain language, permit **I don’t know** and **Skip this**, save progress locally, and avoid showing the whole process as a demanding form. Steps 1 and 2 use neutral blank writing areas without suggested answers. Step 4 groups an expanded set of **feelings**, **body reactions**, and **urges**. Step 5 offers broader emotional, relational, practical, and protective needs. Step 6 asks **What do you want to do next?** and uses literal actions: wait before acting, ask what happened, tell them what is needed, write without sending, help the feeling pass first, do one small practical thing, or return later. At the end, show the assembled picture for review and allow any part to be revised before choosing what to do.

Courtney identified this as an especially important core route within **I’m spiralling**, not an optional journalling extra. It should guide her one step at a time through:

1. **What happened?** — observable events only, with examples and a skip option.
2. **What did my mind say it meant?** — interpretations, predictions, and feared meanings.
3. **What is known, unknown, or being predicted?** — reality-testing without dismissing legitimate concerns.
4. **What is happening in me?** — feelings, body state, urges, and impulses.
5. **What might I need?** — reassurance, information, space, repair, closeness, rest, protection, or another need.
6. **What now?** — pause, ask directly, draft without sending, carry through the peak, take one small action, or return later.

The route must not presume Courtney is mistaken, minimise genuine hurt or unmet needs, decide who is right, or use botanical calmness as decorative gaslighting. Internal fear and a real relationship problem can coexist. Offer **Explain why**, **Make this easier**, **Try something else**, and optional **Ask Binch** support without requiring AI.

### Wait before I do anything

Courtney approved this as the first functional ending from the understanding flow. A tested implementation is integrated into the clickable prototype: choosing **Wait before I do anything** reveals **Set up the pause**, followed by three setup screens, a review screen, and a persistent active-pause state. It supports untimed holds and optional 10, 20, 30, or 60 minute in-page countdowns. Verified behaviours include branching from the assembled summary, multi-selection, default return condition, timer selection/countdown, localStorage restoration after reload, choice review, continuation, and clean ending.

1. **What are you putting on hold?** — sending messages or calling, continuing an argument, making a relationship decision, spending or ordering, posting online, leaving or going somewhere, or something else.
2. **What would make the pause easier?** — hide the draft, put the phone down, move to another room, choose a carry-me-through activity, write the urge without acting, or ask Binch.
3. **How should the pause end?** — return when Courtney chooses, after an optional short timer, after sleeping or resting, after receiving more information, or with no decision yet.

**Return when I choose** is the default. Timers and notifications are never imposed; a notification may occur only when Courtney explicitly creates one. The route should preserve drafts locally, make deliberate action easier than impulsive action, and never punish Courtney for ending or changing the pause.

## Installable private web app direction

Courtney prefers Understory as a private-use Home Screen web app rather than pursuing a native App Store build at this stage. No password or login is required. Privacy means the URL is unlisted and all entries/settings remain local to Courtney’s browser; anyone with the URL could open a blank copy but could not access Courtney’s local data.

The first installable PWA layer is implemented and deployed. It includes `manifest.webmanifest`, standalone display metadata, 192px/512px/Apple touch icons, a service worker that caches the core interface for offline use, and a global Settings button. Automated PWA checks pass and live verification confirmed the manifest, icons, service-worker registration, and Settings rendering.

The initial PWA build exposed only the spiralling/understanding prototype rather than the wider app. After Courtney reported this, the shell was corrected to open on a functional Home with separate **I’m spiralling**, **Help me understand what happened**, **My journal**, and **Settings** routes. My journal can save and remove local pieces. The spiral hub connects pause-before-acting, familiar carry-through options, understanding, closeness/reassurance, and Ask Binch. Automated shell tests and manual navigation/localStorage checks pass.

Courtney subsequently chose to scrap notifications. All notification Settings controls, permission requests, and notification-specific copy were removed. Settings now covers local privacy/storage, installation status, offline use, and confirmed clearing of local data. The service-worker cache was bumped so installed copies can receive the corrected shell.

The PWA provides a web-app manifest, standalone Home Screen launch, service-worker offline support, local-only storage, and a Settings area. No notification capability is planned unless Courtney explicitly revisits that decision.

## Next approved development direction

Courtney requested three connected revisions:

1. Put a large textured coffee-stained paper sheet around the **answer/interaction area**, not around the main question. Courtney clarified this with an annotated Home screenshot: the Understory wordmark/question remains above, while descriptions, choices, fields, and controls sit together on an irregular sheet. The implementation now applies that structure automatically across renderers using a reusable `answerPaper` wrapper with fibrous texture, restrained coffee marks, rugged polygon edges, and a soft shadow.
2. **Carry me through the feeling is implemented as an interactive guided route.** It explores the current feeling/body state/urge, immediate trigger, possible contributors, and feared meaning. A local deterministic brancher produces possible explanations, immediate support, and longer-term ways to work with recurring patterns. It explicitly avoids diagnosis/verdicts and states that internal alarm and a genuine external problem can coexist. A one-minute guided screen changes prompts every 15 seconds and offers return/switch controls.
3. **My journal is implemented as a zoomable, pannable, clickable radial node network matching Courtney’s supplied reference.** Courtney is the large centre node; eight colour-coded category nodes radiate outward; saved traits extend from categories; green good-outcome and rust bad-outcome dots extend from each trait. Thin lines show the hierarchy. The SVG canvas supports drag/pan, wheel zoom, two-finger pinch zoom, plus/minus controls, and reset. Clicking or keyboard-opening any dot shows its type, full text, and directly connected nodes. Trait nodes can be edited or removed with confirmation. Existing local entries drive the graph and older plain journal pieces remain visible.
4. **Pause-before-acting now contains an interactive locked draft box.** Pause step-two options that merely told Courtney to stop using the app—**Hide the draft**, **Put my phone down**, and **Move to another room**—were removed. Selecting **Write what I want to do without doing it** opens an actual editor tied to the held action. Locking stores the text locally, removes it from view, and presents a sealed-box screen with continue, deliberate reopen/revise, and confirmed delete controls. A Home shortcut remains while the draft is locked. “Locked” is explicitly a behavioural/visual pause rather than cryptographic encryption or a physical prevention mechanism.

The journal graph should **not** classify the underlying part of Courtney as good or bad. Courtney clarified that each part/trait should branch into the **good and bad things that come from that part of her**. Structure: `Me → category → part/trait → good consequences + bad/painful consequences`. A trait may therefore remain whole and mixed. Suggested visual coding: sage/moss/pale blue for good consequences; rust/bark/muted plum for bad or painful consequences; cream/olive for the core trait before judgment. Each side may hold several tappable leaves with explanations or memories.

## Current revision feedback

Courtney found the first zoomable journal graph difficult to operate: graph/text movement did not stay centred and the interaction was not comfortable on a phone. Treat that implementation as unapproved. The next graph revision must use a stable centred viewport, focal-point zoom, constrained pan, generous invisible tap targets, tap-to-centre behaviour, a fixed non-moving detail panel, a Back to Me control, and suppression of page scrolling while manipulating the canvas.

Courtney also found the current **I’m spiralling** experience unclear and repetitive. Treat the multi-wizard structure as unapproved. The carry-through route duplicated the understanding route by asking another series of feeling/trigger/meaning questions, and the locked draft was buried too deeply in pause setup. Courtney’s requested replacement is now implemented. **I’m spiralling** begins with one open **Tell me what’s happening** field. That saved local text is shared across distinct support paths. The impulsive path goes directly from naming what is on hold to the functional draft editor; optional timing comes only after the draft is locked. The carry path begins with immediate low-demand support and contains no repeated trigger/meaning questionnaire. The understanding path reuses the shared context as its event and begins at the next distinct step. Later-action drafting and reviewed Ask Binch context also reuse the same entry.

Courtney’s real iPhone video showed that the graph screen still scrolled vertically and that tap-to-focus made the network jump around. The interaction was simplified again: free drag and pinch were removed; the journal stage uses a higher-specificity no-scroll/no-touch-pan rule; tapping a dot changes only the stable detail panel; explicit plus/minus buttons alone change zoom around the selected node; and **Me** restores the complete map. This supersedes the earlier focal-pinch design.

The journal map’s botanical metaphor is now one organism: **Me is a single central marbled pothos**, and every category, part, and outcome grows from that same pothos through its own branching vines. Do not introduce string of pearls, string of hearts, or unrelated plants into the graph. Category vines grow from the central pothos; parts grow as smaller offshoots; helpful outcomes use healthy green leaves or new growth; painful outcomes use dry, curled, rusted, or fallen leaves. The trait/part itself remains neutral—the leaf condition applies only to outcomes.

## Binch / AI integration decision

Courtney wants access to Binch when she needs more detailed advice, has questions, or feels confused during the spiralling pathway. Use a staged approach:

- Understory’s core guidance remains deterministic, useful, and available offline without AI.
- Version one may include an optional **Ask Binch** handoff that opens Courtney’s existing Telegram conversation with Binch.
- The app must not silently send journal entries, emotional selections, screen state, or other private content. It may offer a short context summary for Courtney to review and deliberately copy/share.
- Embedded in-app Binch chat is deferred until it can be implemented properly with authentication, secure session routing, explicit per-item sharing controls, clear online/offline states, cost and availability handling, and preservation of Courtney’s established Hermes profile/context.
- Understory must not imply that Binch guarantees safety, replaces professional or emergency support, or infers danger reliably from private writing.

## Visual direction

Use a **light, tranquil botanical field-journal** aesthetic with pale waterside watercolour, warm fibrous paper, restrained houseplants, and darker moss/bark/rust accents.

Core palette and materials:

- Charcoal and near-black backgrounds.
- Moss, fern, muted sage, dried-flower, and aged-paper accents.
- Pressed plants, taped photographs, handwritten annotations, and restrained tactile texture.
- Atmospheric and personal without becoming visually theatrical or Halloween-themed.

Adapt visual density to capacity:

## AI boundary

The initial app should contain **no built-in AI assistant, chatbot, automated relationship interpretation, or generated emotional advice**.

Courtney currently prefers to use Binch separately when she wants conversational help. The app should remain a predictable, private, offline-capable toolbox and personal archive.

It must never silently process private entries or become the authority on another person's motives, Courtney's diagnosis, or what is real.
